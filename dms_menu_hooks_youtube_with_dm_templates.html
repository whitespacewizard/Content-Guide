LinkedIn DM Selling System

A simple visual map of the DM process from the transcript. The core idea is: start with warm prospects, qualify the right people, create a clear gap between their current situation and desired outcome, then offer help and move naturally to a call.

The whole process in one line Warm Intent → Qualify → Discover Gap → Offer Help → Book Call Every message should feel like the next natural thing to say, not like a rigid script.

1. The DM Flow

This is the operating sequence. The purpose of the early messages is not to pitch. It is to earn a response, understand the buyer, and only move forward when there is a real fit.

2. The “Curiosity Gap”

The transcript describes the main job of the DM conversation as making the gap clear: where the prospect is now, where they want to be, and what is missing between the two.

Present Reality

  • Pipeline is inconsistent
  • Too dependent on referrals
  • Lead quality is weak
  • Growth is hard to scale
THE GAP
Your offer only matters after the gap is understood.

Desired State

  • Predictable pipeline
  • 3–5 qualified clients per month
  • Better quality opportunities
  • Less founder dependence
Discover reality “What are you focused on right now?”
Expand the pain “What have you seen recently that made this a priority?”
Expose the gap “What do you think is missing from getting you there?”

3. Example DM Conversation

This should feel like a real conversation, not a qualification checklist. Notice how each message reacts to the prospect's last reply instead of jumping straight to the next scripted question.

Prospect Yeah, pretty much. We get some inbound too, but referrals are still the main thing.
You Makes sense. Has that been working fine, or is the inconsistency starting to get annoying?
Prospect Honestly, the inconsistency is the issue. Some months are great, then it suddenly goes quiet.
You Yeah, that’s the frustrating part. Especially when the team has capacity but you can’t really predict what’s coming in.
Prospect Exactly. We could probably handle another 3 or 4 clients right now.
You Got you. What have you tried so far to make that more predictable?
Prospect Mostly content and a bit of outbound. We get engagement, but we’re not really doing much with it.
You Ah, got it. So the attention is there, it’s more that there isn’t a proper follow-up system behind it.
Prospect Yeah, that’s probably the biggest gap.
You That’s actually very similar to what we see with a lot of agencies. We usually turn that warm engagement into a simple DM follow-up process instead of going straight to cold outreach.
You Happy to show you what that looks like if you want. Might be easier than me writing a novel in here 😄
Prospect Yeah, sure. Send it over.

Pitch Slap

Endless Casual Chat

Keep talking forever without ever surfacing a problem, offering help, or creating a conversion event.

AEO: How to Get Recommended by AI Search

Core idea: Traditional SEO is the foundation, but AI visibility also depends on topic-wide relevance, fresh structured content, third-party mentions, YouTube, crawlability, and measurement.
  • AI uses query fan-out: one user prompt can become many smaller searches, so you need authority across an entire topic rather than ranking for only one keyword.
  • Focus on consensus, freshness, and authority: consistent brand mentions across the web help AI trust the story, fresh content gets cited more often, and strong traditional Google rankings still give you an advantage.
  • Do a brand-gap and prompt analysis: find where competitors are mentioned but you are not, where your brand is mentioned without being cited, and which topics AI associates with competitors instead of you.
  • Make content easy for AI to quote: put the bottom line first, make each section understandable on its own, use specific entities and relationships, and write clear declarative sentences. Keep important pages fresh.

  • Check the technical and measurement layer: make sure robots.txt is not blocking AI crawlers, then track AI referral traffic, AI bot activity, and self-reported attribution such as “How did you hear about us?”

LinkedIn Cold DM Blueprint: Voice Notes + ABCD Method

Core idea: Cold LinkedIn DMs work better when you reduce friction, respect the prospect’s attention, and use formats that feel more human and easier to reply to.
  • Use voice notes as a pattern interrupt: most LinkedIn inboxes are full of generic text, so voice notes stand out and create curiosity.
  • Keep voice notes casual and human: quickly introduce yourself, explain why you sent a voice note, and share one relevant idea instead of delivering a stiff pitch.
  • Respect the attention power dynamic: you are entering their inbox and asking for something, so make the message easy to read and easy to respond to.
  • Do not ask for a meeting too early: the first objective is a low-friction response, not immediately forcing a call or demo.
  • Use the ABCD method for text DMs: let the prospect respond with one letter, which reduces effort and can improve reply rates.
  • ABCD also improves CRM segmentation: A = ready, B = later follow-up, C = not interested, D = definitely not interested.

Template 1: Voice Note DM

Hi [Name], sorry for the voice note. I’m just on the move at the moment, but I really wanted to reach out to you.

I’m [Your Name] from [Business].

I had an idea around [specific problem / opportunity] that I thought might be useful for you.

Here’s what I’m thinking: [short useful idea / observation].

If that sounds relevant, happy to explain it properly.

Template 2: ABCD Text DM

Hi [Name], I’m [Your Name] from [Business].

I’ll use as little of your time as possible.

Could you reply with just one letter?

A = Yes, I’m interested. Let’s move forward.
B = Interested, but not right now.
C = Not really interested.
D = F*** off, leave me alone 😄

No pressure either way.
